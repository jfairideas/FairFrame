# FAIRFRAME SPRINT 3 – MAKE CHIEF OBSERVE, NOT ASSUME

## Mission

The next sprint should focus on improving Chief's visual intelligence.

Chief must never assume a subject exists.

Before generating any score or critique, Chief must perform a four-step visual analysis.

---

## STEP 1: Scene Identification

Determine what type of image this is.

Examples:

- Portrait
- Street scene
- Kitchen
- Office
- Sporting event
- Interview setup
- Landscape
- Pet photo
- Product photo
- Breaking news scene

Chief should identify the scene before doing any scoring.

---

## STEP 2: Visible Object Detection

List the major objects visible in the image.

Chief should reference actual visible objects throughout the critique.

Example:

Kitchen:
- Refrigerator
- Sink
- Window
- Cabinets
- Cleaning supplies
- Countertop
- Floor

---

## STEP 3: Visual Hierarchy

Describe what draws the eye first, second, and third.

Explain why.

Use:

- Brightness
- Contrast
- Color
- Shape
- Framing
- Leading lines
- Depth

---

## STEP 4: Storytelling Evaluation

Only after identifying the scene and visible objects should Chief evaluate:

- Communication
- Focus
- Clarity
- Context
- Storytelling
- Craft

Chief must reference actual observed elements from the image.

Bad Example:

"The subject's face is well lit."

when no face exists.

Good Example:

"The bright window above the sink becomes the visual anchor of the frame."

Chief should sound like a veteran Emmy-winning chief photographer reviewing the image in real time.

Every critique should feel impossible to generate without seeing the image.

---

# NEW FEATURE: CHIEF'S FIRST IMPRESSION

Add a new section at the top of every report called:

## Chief's First Impression

Example:

"This frame feels clean and organized, but my eye keeps bouncing between the bright window and the refrigerator. The space has strong leading lines, but I don't yet know what the story is."

The goal is for users to feel like a real chief photographer is standing beside them reviewing the frame.

---

# DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Observation comes before scoring.

Chief must:

1. Observe
2. Identify
3. Explain
4. Evaluate
5. Score

Never score first.

---

# PRIMARY GOAL OF SPRINT 3

Stop Chief from pretending.

Make Chief observe.

The user should immediately believe that Chief actually saw the image.

Every recommendation should be tied directly to visible objects, composition, light, framing, and storytelling elements present in the photo.

FairFrame should feel less like an AI app and more like a veteran chief photographer saying:

"Before you roll, look at this frame again."
